Product Strategy Template

**Document Purpose:** This quick reference guide is designed to help you transform a collection of data points into a persuasive narrative. A great product strategy isn't just a document; it is a tool used to align stakeholders and secure resources for your vision.

0\. Executive Summary
---------------------

**Purpose:** Set context and make the meeting outcome clear.

**PMs should include:**

*   Why we’re here (decision, alignment, or approval needed)
    
*   The core problem (who is affected, how, and why now)The opportunity (customer + business value)
    
*   Preview of 3–5 strategic investment areas
    
*   Explicit asks / discussion topicsDefinition of success (customer + business metrics
    

**Great looks like**

*   Crisp, decision-oriented, outcome-focused
    
*   Clear ask and recommendation
    
*   Strategy-level framing (not feature lists)
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Generic summary of the document
    
*   No decision or ask
    
*   Vague problems or success metrics
    

1\. Assessment
==============

The Product Assessment is the foundation of a strong product strategy. Its purpose is to move from evidence → insight → choices → investment → outcomes. This is not a research archive. Deep analysis lives in linked docs. This artifact synthesizes the so what.

**1.1 The Problem (User + Company Framing)**
--------------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Clearly articulate the problem worth solving.

**Frame from three perspectives:**

*   **Primary user (C1):** unmet jobs, friction, failure modes, workarounds
    
*   **Secondary user (C2):** downstream experience and trust impact
    
*   **Company:** why this matters (growth, retention, brand, platform leverage)
    

**Support with evidence:**

*   Qualitative: interviews, usability, CS/sales insights
    
*   Quantitative: funnel metrics, adoption, time-to-value, retention, NPS drivers
    
*   Severity: frequency × impact × strategic importance
    

**Great looks like**

*   Problem is specific, evidenced, and material, outlined from a customer perspective
    
*   Quant & qual blacked evidence 
    
*   Clear articulation of what working vs not
    
*   Business impact is explicit
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Solution-shaped problem statements
    
*   Anecdotal evidence only
    
*   No clarity on scope or severity
    

**1.2 Marketing & Industry Landscape**
--------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Explain what’s changing externally and why timing matters.

**Include:**

*   Industry and category trends
    
*   Customer behavior and expectation shifts
    
*   Technology shifts (AI, automation, platforms, privacy, data)
    
*   Macro or regulatory forces
    
*   A synthesized number of important trends with direct product implications.
    

**Great looks like**

*   Clear “why now”
    
*   Trends tied directly to product strategy choices
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Market research dump
    
*   Long lists with no implications
    

**1.3 Competitive Assessment**
------------------------------

**Purpose:** Set the bar and understand where value is won or lost.

**Include:**

*   Competitive set (direct, indirect, alternatives)
    
*   Market positioning and GTM models
    
*   Key product and platform experiences
    
*   Investment signals and momentum
    
*   User sentiment and pain points
    

**Great looks like**

*   Insight into competitor intent and strategy
    
*   Clear understanding of where competitors are winning and why
    
*   Where does your offering rank in comparison? Both customer usage and sentiment/value
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Feature-by-feature comparison tables
    
*   No synthesis or conclusions
    

**1.4 Synthesis & Strategic Implications (Including SWOT)**
-----------------------------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Turn analysis into insight and opportunity.

**Synthesis should cover:**

*   5–7 key insights from the problem, market, and competition
    
*   Implications for:
    
    *   Users (value creation)
        
    *   Product direction
        
    *   Company strategy and economics
        
*   **Consider SWOT (concise and decision-driven):**
    
    *   Strengths: durable assets you can leverage
        
    *   Weaknesses: structural constraints or experience debt
        
    *   Opportunities: unmet needs or shifts you can capitalize on
        
    *   Threats: competitors, substitutes, or systemic risks
        
*   **Output:** Clear opportunity areas and strategic posture (where to lead, where to match, where to avoid).
    

**Great looks like**

*   Clear “so what” and opportunity framing
    
*   SWOT used to drive choices
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Repetition of earlier sections
    
*   Generic or exhaustive SWOT lists
    

**1.5 Current User Experience (Where It Breaks)**
-------------------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Create shared understanding of today’s pain.

**Include:**

*   1–2 critical user flows tied to key outcomes
    
*   Annotated journey/screens showing friction and breakdowns
    
*   Where value leaks (time, trust, accuracy, confidence)
    
*   Common workarounds users adopt
    

**Great looks like**

*   Concrete, visceral representation of pain
    
*   Clear linkage to outcomes and metrics
    

**Weak looks like**

*   High-level journey maps
    
*   UI critiques without outcome impact
    

2\. The Strategy
================

The Strategy is the go-forward recommendation with sound rationale & perspective. The future with clear investment and impact. Please note not all strategies are must do - successful strategy many times can be a recommendation of what not to do.  

**2.1 Vision & Mission**
------------------------

**Purpose:** Provide long-term direction and alignment.

**Include:**

*   Vision: the future state you’re working toward
    
*   Mission: how the product will get there
    
*   Rationale for key words or phrases
    
*   Outline the opportunity!
    
*   Explicit non-goals and tradeoffs
    

**Great looks like**

*   Helps teams make decisions and say no
    
*   Aspirational but grounded
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Buzzwords or marketing language
    
*   No clarity on tradeoffs
    

**2.2 Future Experience & Value**
---------------------------------

**Purpose:** Make the strategy tangible.

**Include:**

*   A future-state user story (C1 and/or C2)
    
*   Prototype, storyboard, or narrative walkthrough
    
*   Moments of disproportionate value creation
    
*   Clear contrast with today
    
*   You like should outline the target audience as part of this with why and opportunity
    

**Great looks like**

*   Easy to summarize in one sentence
    
*   Focused on outcomes, not UI polish
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Vague future descriptions
    
*   Overly UI-focused demos
    

**2.3 Themed User Stories**
---------------------------

**Purpose:** Bridge vision to execution.

**For each user story:**

*   Today: current pain and behavior
    
*   Tomorrow: desired outcome
    
*   Investment needed (product, platform, data, GTM)
    
*   Cost level and dependencies
    
*   Value unlocked (customer + business metrics)
    

**Great looks like**

*   Outcome-oriented themes
    
*   Clear value-to-investment logic
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Backlog items grouped together
    
*   No metric or value clarity
    

**2.3 Strategic Investment Areas (3–5)**
----------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Define the big bets that guide prioritization.

**Each investment area should clarify:**

*   What problem it addresses and for whom
    
*   Why it matters strategically
    
*   Why you are positioned to win
    
*   What’s in scope and out of scope
    
*   How success is measured
    

**Great looks like**

*   Clear, defensible bets
    
*   Explicit tradeoffs
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Too many investment areas
    
*   Vague or overlapping themes
    

**2.4 Differentiation & Competitive Advantage**
-----------------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Explain why customers choose and stay with you.

**Include:**

*   Primary differentiators that matter to users
    
*   Sources of advantage (data, ecosystem, trust, distribution, scale)
    
*   How the advantage compounds over time
    
*   Intentional tradeoffs
    

**Great looks like**

*   Differentiation visible in product and roadmap
    
*   Hard to copy quickly
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Parity features framed as differentiation
    
*   Reliance on brand alone
    

**2.5 Goals & Key Metrics**
---------------------------

**Purpose:** Define how success will be measured.

**Include:**

*   North-star metric tied to customer value
    
*   Leading indicators (activation, adoption, time-to-value, quality)
    
*   Business outcomes (retention, growth, revenue)
    
*   Guardrails (reliability, compliance, support)
    

**Great looks like**

*   Metrics reflect value creation
    
*   Clear hierarchy and ownership
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Output metrics only
    
*   Too many unprioritized metrics
    

**2.6 Product Roadmap (1–2 Years)**
-----------------------------------

**Purpose:** Show sequencing and learning.

**Include:**

*   Roadmap organized by strategic themes
    
*   Quarterly or half-year milestones
    
*   Expected impact on key metrics
    
*   Tradeoffs, dependencies, and discovery points
    

**Great looks like**

*   Communicates intent and priorities
    
*   Leaves room for learning
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Feature lists with dates
    
*   Over-committed plans
    

**2.7 Risks & Dependencies**
----------------------------

**Purpose:** Build confidence through transparency.

**Include:**

*   Key risks (technical, data, GTM, adoption, legal)
    
*   Critical dependencies (teams, platforms, partners)
    
*   Mitigation plans, owners, and status
    

**Great looks like**

*   Risks surfaced early with mitigation
    
*   Dependencies actively managed
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Risks minimized or buried
    
*   No ownership or mitigation
    

**2.8 Decisions, Next Steps & Asks**
------------------------------------

**Purpose:** Drive alignment and action.

**Include:**

*   Decisions needed from leadership
    
*   Options considered and recommendation
    
*   Next steps (30/60/90 days)
    
*   Explicit asks (resources, alignment, approvals)
    

**Great looks like**

*   Meeting ends with clear commitments
    
*   Owners and timelines are explicit
    

**Weak looks like**

*   Ends with “discussion”
    
*   No clear outcomes
